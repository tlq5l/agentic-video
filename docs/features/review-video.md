# Review video

How a user reaches it: the `review_video` tool.

Code: `src/executor/mcp_server.py` (the `review_video` branch of `_dispatch`, `_loudness_summary`). It calls `AgenticVideoEngine.probe`, the `contact_sheet` and `detect_cuts` dispatch branches, and `AgenticVideoEngine.loudness_timeline`. The black scan is `black_ranges` in `src/media/visual_timing.py`, which uses the same near-black test as `blank_flashes`. Its 160x90 grey frames come from the cut scan's own decode (`scan_scene_changes(grid_rate=...)` and `brightest_levels` in `src/media/scene.py`), so the video is decoded once for both.

How to check it works: `pytest tests/test_review_video.py tests/test_schema_contract.py tests/test_agent_first_tools.py`.

Result fields:
- `black_ranges`: stretches of at least 0.2 s where the picture is near-black, in time order, each with `start_sec`, `end_sec`, `duration_sec`. At most 50 are listed (`black_ranges_omitted` says how many were left out).
- `black_total`: how many near-black stretches were found in all.
- `loudness.silence_gaps` (with `silence_gaps_total`) already lists silent stretches, so there is no separate silence field. It uses the `loudness_timeline` silence test (below -60 dBFS for at least 30 ms).

Near-black means the brightest 1% of a frame's 160x90 grey cells stay below 40 (the same `_BLANK_P99` that `blank_flashes` uses). A dim scene that keeps some bright cells is not listed. A fade to black is listed from the point where it gets below that level.

Gotchas:
- The whole call is meant to finish in under 45 s (Executor cuts calls at 60 s). The cut scan is split into parts on long files, and the grid levels are taken from those parts with the same seams, so a long clip needs one decode for both views. Each part's grid starts at its own seek point (`start_time=0` on the grid filter), so its rows line up with the seconds it owns. A part whose first decoded frame comes after its seam (a timestamp gap across its seek point) is not used: the whole file is decoded once instead, so the result matches a cuts-first call. A fractional frame rate (29.97) is decoded as one part: exact, but slower.
- Gap clip, measured 2026-10-31 on the Cloudflare box (4 cores), 250 s 30 fps clip with a 1.5 s timestamp gap across the 63 s seam, cold, two runs each: before this change 2.4 s and 2.4 s (split decode; black stretch 63.0-64.0 s, which disagrees with a cuts-first call); after it 4.7 s and 4.2 s (one decode; black stretch 63.2-64.0 s, the same as a cuts-first call).
- Timing, measured 2026-10-31 on the Cloudflare box (4 cores), one cold run each, 10-minute 1080p30 H.264 clip: `review_video` 32.8 s and 34.8 s on two runs. The same branch before the shared decode took 54.5 s on two runs. `detect_cuts` alone 23.6 s, against 23.0 s at the base commit 59fdadf. The cut scan with the grid output 27.2 s. On the 10 s 1000 fps 720p clip `review_video` takes 12.6 s, against 11.8 s before this change (no gain there: its scan has one part, and the extra grid output costs about what the separate black decode did). Repeat calls on the same file come from cache (`black_ranges_v2` in the media cache). The 45 s check in `tests/test_review_video.py` applies to the 60 s fixture only.
- The black scan and the contact sheet run in parallel; `detect_cuts` runs after the black scan, reads the scene cache it fills, and settles the same changes as before. If the scene cache already holds the cut scan (a `detect_cuts` call ran earlier), no grid is produced and the black scan decodes the grid itself, as before.
- Black-scan method, same clip, one run each: the repo's 160x90 frame reader 27.3 s; ffmpeg `blackdetect` on a 160x90 grey scale 33.6 s; ffmpeg `blackdetect` at full size 31.8 s. The repo reader is the fastest, so it is used.
- Accuracy, measured 2026-10-31 on 30 generated 640x360 clips: 14 with 1-3 planted black stretches of 0.2-2 s, 6 with a 1 s fade to black followed by a black hold, and 10 dark but not black (a dim test pattern, dim colour bars, a night-like noisy grey, a dim gradient). For the 36 planted and fade stretches, precision and recall at IoU 0.5 or more are both 1.0, and all 20 of those clips list exactly their stretches. The 10 dark clips list none; their brightest-1% levels run from 43 to 103 against the 40 threshold, and the closest clips (dim test pattern, night-like grey) sit at 43-44, so a clip only a little darker would be listed. The first run dimmed the mandelbrot clips at -0.6, which left their brightest-1% level at 3-14 (darker than video black), so they were near-black and got listed; they were redone at -0.3 (levels 91-103). Before this change the result had no black field, so recall was 0. The clips are synthetic; real dark scenes are not measured.
- No audio track: `loudness` is null and `note` says so. Any step failing fails the whole call with that step's error; nothing is returned half-filled. If FFmpeg exits with an error while decoding frames, the call fails with `MediaCorruptError` (the exit code is in its details), and a partly decoded scan is neither listed nor cached.
- `loudness` is a summary (10 strongest outliers, 20 silence gaps). `average_rms_dbfs` is computed from all windows, so it does not depend on those limits.

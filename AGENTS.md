# Agent Guide: Video Reasoning with `agentic_video`

This document provides operational principles, tool heuristics, and anti-patterns for text+vision AI agents using the `agentic_video` capability.

Feature map: FEATURES.md. Open it for bug reports, vague requests, or before changing a feature.

---

## The Core Interaction Protocol

When an agent is tasked with answering questions about a video, **never scan the entire video frame-by-frame**. Always follow the **Think → Act → Observe** loop:

```text
1. PROBE       ──> Check duration, resolution, stream types, and audio presence.
                   `review_video({ source })` does this plus a contact sheet, cuts and a loudness summary in one call.
2. TRIAGE      ──> Decide if the question is acoustic (speech/dialogue) or visual.
3. FORAGE      ──> If acoustic, query transcript first (0 vision tokens).
                   If visual, generate a coarse overview contact sheet (1 image, ~258 tokens).
4. LOCALIZE    ──> Narrow the temporal hypothesis to a candidate window [t_start, t_end].
5. RESAMPLE    ──> Extract targeted dense frames (2–4 FPS) only in the target window.
6. GROUND      ──> Cite exact millisecond timecodes and frame badges in the answer.
```

## Tool Selection Heuristics

### 1. Acoustic & Spoken Content
- **Question Examples**: *"What did the speaker say about X?"*, *"When is concept Y mentioned?"*, *"Did anyone say word Z?"*
- **Action**:
  1. Call `search_transcript({ source, query: "..." })`.
  2. If found, use `start_sec` and `end_sec` to zoom directly to the moment.
  3. If not found, **STOP immediately** for negative queries. Do not waste vision tokens searching frames for spoken words.

### 2. Non-speech Audio & Sync
- For music, hits, attacks, or audio/video alignment, measure first with `audio_onsets`, `loudness_timeline`, and `sync_check`.
- Then inspect the suspicious time range with `spectrogram_window`, adding cue or cut markers when useful.
- Use `band_hz` to isolate pitched events and `compare_to` to find added events against a reference; give `sync_check` a cue band for frequency-specific sounds.
- Judge loudness outliers by their `direction`, `shape`, and `fault_level`; describe ramps and transients as informational, and use `ignore_ranges` for known intentional sections.
- Never guess timings by ear; use the returned measurements and burned timecodes.

## Self-running check loop

Use `python -m src.loop check <video>` to measure sync, loudness, onsets, and cuts, then review the evidence in its `report.md` and `evidence/` directory. Both `check --judge` and the run config `judge` default to `none`; opt into Jev with `--judge jev` or `"judge": "jev"`. Jev uses text and measurements only and cannot inspect frames. Cue labels are grouped by their leading sound kind. Repeated cues are checked against a short time-frequency pattern learned from their instances and comparison beats without that group. This aligns a swell to its typical start instead of timing its energy peak. Search stays within the cue's group slot and a beat-aware window; only strong evidence extends the window to recover a displaced sound. Search-edge or weak peaks cannot establish early/late. Peaks are assigned once per group across neighbouring cues, and duplicate label/time entries collapse to one cue. A cue fault is confirmed only on dual evidence: the template matcher and a simple band check must agree. The simple check looks for an attack within tolerance in the group's dominant third-octave band (one not claimed by a co-occurring group), no more than 10 dB below the group's typical level, rising over the preceding 90 ms, and at least 8 dB above what nearby comparison moments plus the co-occurring groups' typical contributions explain. The template matcher likewise removes a co-occurring group's louder-than-usual energy (an accented kick) before learning and scoring, and reports a credible displaced instance in the cue's slot as early/late rather than missing. If the simple check finds the sound on time, the cue is `ok`, or `ambiguous` when the template instead has a credible displaced peak. Early/late also needs the simple check to see the displaced attack. Each cue problem names its exact label, group, offset, score, method, and both checks' results; a group-wide detectable-attack lag is informational. `ambiguous` cues go under “Needs review”, reach the fixer as review items, and never count as confirmed faults. Time ranges in the `--intent` notes (for example "from 12.5 s to 15.0 s", "12.5–15 s", "between 12.5 and 15 seconds", "0:12.5 - 0:15") mark sections as expected to be altered; ranges are half-open, so a cue at or within one tolerance of the end is judged normally. Cues inside them are informational unless no attack is found in any band, and loudness outliers there are ignored. An early match that lands inside an intent section describing a riser or swell (it builds into the cue) goes to “Needs review”, since that sound may be the swell itself; other sections, such as a muffled bridge, do not excuse a misplaced sound. When no band stands out beyond a cue's companions, the checker measures the next two beats in four equal windows, then, if that finds no build, up to four beats in eight windows (stopping at the next cue of the same group or the file end, so a slow riser counts), using the cue's reliable dominant band or broadband when none is available. It compares that rise with matching companion moments and the same span just before the cue. A steady relative rise of at least 6 dB, ending at least 3 dB above the comparison moments and at least 3 dB stronger than the pre-cue span, with increases of at least 0.5 dB in at least two steps, marks a soft-attack sound for review. If there are too few comparison moments to measure it, rising-sound words in the cue label or a relevant intent note can break the tie in favour of review; words alone do not override a measured result. When the measurement is conclusive and finds no build, the cue remains missing. A cue reported missing whose own band is within 3 dB of the group's usual level also goes to “Needs review”. A repeated cue with only one other instance is compared with that instance too: if its own band is more than 10 dB below it, a matching transient alone cannot make it present. In a group of three or more, a cue with no simple-check attack whose own band is a clear outlier below the other instances (at least 3 dB, or 6 dB with only two peers, and three scaled deviations of their spread) is reported missing too. A one-off cue (its group has no other instance, so no signature or usual level) landing with other cues is reported missing when no band at it rises 8 dB above the median of nearby moments with the same companion cues; if it lands within one tolerance of where an intent section begins or ends (a breath, a dropout), those moments do not compare and it goes to “Needs review” instead. Ranges may name their end point (“from 8.75 s to the reveal at 10 s”), and explicit seconds make a range count even after words like “beats”. A cue in the first 0.12 s has no pre-roll to show an attack: if its group's band is at its usual level there, it goes to “Needs review” instead of missing. When two cues at the same time both match at the same moved spot, and at least 80% of one group's cues coincide with the other, larger group (every whoosh comes with a text pop), the move is blamed on the smaller group; the larger group's cue goes to “Needs review”, because its learned pattern carries the other sound. The reverse also happens: a stacked cue's pattern can "match" on time from its companions alone. When the template calls a cue on time but its group's own band there is more than 10 dB below the group's usual level, and a clear attack of that band sits elsewhere in the cue slot (away from the group's usual attack lag, and nearer this cue than any other cue of the group), the cue is reported early/late at that attack. A step-shaped boost in one band is fault-level only at 8 dB or more, or when three or more bands rise together, and not when its extra energy is new content (an arrangement change) rather than the band's usual content turned up. Three checks look past the cue list. An `uncued_sound` is a strong instance of a cue group's sound (at least 85% of the group's usual on-cue template score, its own band within 3 dB of its usual level and attacking there) more than one search window from every cue of that group; a peak on another cue whose sound may explain it (same band, similar signature, or no learned signature), or on a cue's own early/late sound, does not count, and a group with more such peaks than a quarter of its cues is matching the backing, so none count. A `stereo_balance_shift` is two or more bars where one octave band's right-minus-left level sits 6 dB or more off the band's usual balance (where most bars agree), on the same side; if the band is also 6 dB louder there it is new content and informational. A `text_early`/`text_late` applies to cues labelled `"<sound> | <on-screen text>"`: nothing in the picture stands out at the cue (per-frame change on a 160x90 grey grid, against the window's background motion), but a caption-sized change (2% of the frame) appears within 1.5 beats and no nearer another text cue. Intent sections make all three informational. When an uncued candidate fails its group's simple-check band (that band is kept free of sounds that always land with the group, so a ping on every scene change can get a band it barely reaches), it may instead be confirmed in its own attack band: the band where the candidate rises most, skipping bands claimed by other cues at that moment, where the group's own cues also rise 6 dB and within 6 dB of their strongest band, at no less than 3 dB under their usual level. Groups that always sound together can match one uncued sound; it is reported once, naming the others. Five more checks look at the mix and the picture as a whole. A `stereo_off_centre` is a band below 354 Hz whose usual balance sits 6 dB or more to one side in at least half of its bars: low end panned or routed to one channel for the whole piece, which a shift cannot show. A `clipping` run is 100 ms windows with 24 or more samples (either channel) at or above 0.95 of full scale; a lone overshoot is not one, and when such windows cover a fifth of the programme the master is loud throughout and the runs are informational. A clipping run or other range-based loudness problem is excused by an intent section only when more than half its duration lies inside that section; brief overlap leaves the rest reportable. A `text_cropped` applies to timed text cues: 0.3, 0.6 and 0.9 s after the text appears (kept before the next text cue), sharp strokes (40 grey levels off their 31x31 neighbourhood) that sit in the same place in every one of those frames run into a frame edge on 24 or more lines, and the frame just before the text appeared shows fewer than half as many; those lines must also fall in text-like rows (repeated stroke runs over three or more rows), so a solid prop that arrives with the title does not count; a title holds still, while scenery scrolling past the edge does not, and a fixed border already touches it before. Near-edge text is reported as an ambiguous review item when a stable text-like line band at least 16 px tall ends within 2% of an edge (capped at 24 px) and outside the outer 4 px, with fewer than half as many matching near-edge positions in the preceding frame. Row gaps up to 4 px merge into one line band. For a left or right edge, the substantial stroke columns followed inward must keep gaps below 0.6 line height across at least three line heights, so a thin wire or widely spaced round decorations do not count as a text line. Alignment alone never confirms it. A text-only judge cannot inspect frames to tell whether the edge cuts a letter or falls in a gap, so near-edge cases stay review items for the fixer to inspect. A `blank_flash` is at most 0.15 s of near-black frames (brightest 1% under 40 of 255 on the grey grid) between two pictures that are already at their steady brightness, so a fade or a dark scene does not count. A `flash_frame` is one or two frames that differ from both neighbours while the pictures on either side match, so a real cut does not count; frames already reported as a blank flash are not reported again. Intent sections make these informational. A frozen_picture is at least 0.5 s of identical or near-identical frames inside a picture that moves on both sides; still shots and title or end cards lack that surrounding motion, and gradual fades are not held frames. Intent sections make frozen-picture findings informational. With `--judge jev`, Jev evaluates each candidate using the measurements, nearby cues, and matching intent sentence; it may downgrade a rule fault but cannot confirm a rule review item. The report gives Jev's agreement rate. By default, `--judge none` keeps the rule verdicts. If Jev is unavailable, the report records the failure and uses the rules. The command exits 1 when any problem is confirmed.

Use `python -m src.loop run --config loop.json` to build, check, fix the source project, and re-check. Send confirmed problems and review items to the fixer, with each review item's measurements, reason, and contact-sheet or spectrogram paths. The fixer checks each review item itself by opening its evidence and measuring with ffmpeg if needed; it changes source only when it confirms a real, unintended fault, and otherwise leaves it and says why. It ends its reply with one `REVIEW <id>: fault|not a fault — <short reason>` line per review item. Parsed verdicts go to `loop_log.jsonl` and `summary.md`; an item judged `not a fault` with unchanged numbers is not sent again. Review items do not block success. If there are no confirmed problems but unjudged review items remain, run one fixer round for them, then stop. Leave cue and intent files alone. The default ladder is `gpt-6-luna` xhigh, `gpt-6-sol` medium, `gpt-6-sol` high, then `gpt-6-astra` low. If the same problem remains or a build breaks, retry once on the same rung, then climb to the next. If both attempts fail on the last rung, stop with “needs human review” and hand off to Opus. The fixer brief carries the intent notes and lists each intent section as “do not touch”; the fixer addresses confirmed problems and applies the review-item instructions above, reporting a suspected false alarm instead of redesigning or re-levelling a sound. The loop measures each intent section's band levels on the first build; if a later build moves any audible band there (one within 24 dB of the section's loudest band, so noise-floor wobble does not count) by 3 dB or more (`protect_tolerance_db`), it adds an `intent_section_changed` problem asking the fixer to undo it. Windows within 0.5 s of a problem the fixer was asked to fix are left out, so moving a misplaced sound out of a section is not counted as damage (turn off with `"protect_intent_sections": false`). A problem is listed under Fixed problems only after a counted fixer round and a successful later check no longer reports it. If the fixer stops at a usage or rate limit, the loop ends with status “retry later” (exit 75): the attempt is not counted, the ladder does not move, and `loop_log.jsonl` records `fixer_status: "usage_limit"`. Re-run after the limit resets. A fixer that prints its answer instead of writing `{last_message_file}` still leaves it in `last-message.txt`.

### 3. Coarse Timeline & Visual Progression
- **Question Examples**: *"What is this video generally about?"*, *"What happens around the middle?"*
- **Action**:
  1. Call `contact_sheet({ source, samples: 12, cols: 4 })`.
  2. Inspect the single grid image. Read the burned `#idx [HH:MM:SS.mmm]` badges on each tile.
  3. Cost: exactly 1 vision image payload (~258 tokens) instead of 12 separate images (3,096 tokens).

### 4. Sub-Second Visual Precision & High-Speed Actions
- **Question Examples**: *"At what exact second did the light turn red?"*, *"What icon flashed for a fraction of a second?"*
- **Action**:
  1. First identify the 2–5 second candidate window from contact sheets or scene cuts.
  2. Call `inspect_window({ source, start_sec: 14.0, end_sec: 16.0, fps: 4.0 })`.
  3. Read the burned timestamp overlays directly from the high-resolution crop frames.

## Negative Controls & Falsification Safety

When answering negative queries (*"Does an elephant appear in this office meeting?"*):
1. **Never scan 100% of frames blindly** hoping to confirm absence.
2. Check `search_transcript` first. If speech mentions the topic, inspect that segment.
3. If unmentioned, inspect an 8-tile coarse overview contact sheet. If absent in all tiles, conclude absence with confidence.

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- ❌ **Anti-Pattern 1: The Frame Dumper**: Requesting 1.0 FPS across a 10-minute video. This dumps 600 images (154,800 tokens) into context, causing context exhaustion and high latency.
- ❌ **Anti-Pattern 2: The Hallucinated Timestamp**: Guessing timestamps from memory without inspecting the burned timecode badge (`#01 [00:14.250]`) on the image.
- ❌ **Anti-Pattern 3: Visual Search for Audio Events**: Scanning hundreds of visual frames to find where someone said "Good morning". Always use `search_transcript` first.
- ❌ **Anti-Pattern 4: Raw Video Ingestion**: Assuming the LLM has native video byte support. Always pass extracted JPEG frames or contact sheet paths/tool_files.
